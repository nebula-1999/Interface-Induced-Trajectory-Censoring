Tool-use evaluations read their call rates from a serving stack. That rate can be zero while the model emits a complete call that the configured interface does not recognize. On Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard v4, holding weights, cases, decoding, seeds, executor, and scorer fixed, changing the serving adapter moves one model's score under BFCL's official scorer from 0.00 to 0.96 on simple_python and from 0.00 to 0.19 on multi_turn_base. In a 2x2 over template and parser, neither one-component replacement recovers a call from the documented baseline, whereas replacing both recovers 196/200, showing strong complementarity between the two serialization contracts. Across five Qwen2.5-Coder checkpoints under the mismatched interface, the server parses 0/100 calls at every size while independently classified call emissions rise from 0 to 80/100 (approximately 72 after human calibration). Under a matched envelope, a preregistered supporting ladder holds the silent fraction at 0-2 through 8B. On 115 stateful tau-bench retail tasks, the same adapter swap moves parsed and executed calls from 0 to 636 and tasks reaching any tool from 0 to 103. The mismatch also changes RL experience: in a preregistered 150-step 7B LoRA control whose arms differ only in the parser registered inside verl, the mismatched arm converts 8,689 tight-classified emissions into 10 executions, versus 16,844 executions after repair. Held-out rescues nevertheless remain 12 and final passes are 430/542 versus 427/542 (p=0.25): the intervention restores tool-mediated experience, but we detect no held-out improvement at this scale, budget, and seed. The observed tool-call rate is not a property of the model alone; it is a property of the model-interface stack that measures it.
